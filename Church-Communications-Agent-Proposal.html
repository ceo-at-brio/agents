The brief

Why this, and why now

You called bulletin-making the unholy work of being a priest. The point of this agent is to take that work so the priest can visit hospitals, bless people, and be with the congregation.

What changes every week

  • Readings from the Revised Common Lectionary
  • Cover image
  • Songs / hymns
  • Announcements

What stays in the template

  • Order of service and other static liturgy
  • Collect and weekly propers that appear on your samples
  • Parish defaults: name, translation, OneLicense number, season track
  • Two footers: music license and AI disclosure
How the calendar actually asks questions. In ordinary time, if a major feast overlaps a Sunday, the agent asks the question from the call: All Saints' Day or Proper 26? Track 1 vs Track 2 is chosen at the start of the season and stored on the parish. Outside ordinary time — Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter — there is no fork. The agent confirms the day and moves on.

Step 1 · Past leaflets

Show what they already approved, not what they “sent”

The shelf is last year’s matching Sunday and this year’s approved PDFs. Fr. Thomas can start fresh or start from last All Saints, because the reading cycle comes around again.

Drafts and approved stay separate so nobody reprints a half-finished leaflet.

Reuse is the point of the three-year calendar. “Start from this” copies last All Saints into this week’s draft.

The license number is reused every week in the required music footer. Track is changed when ordinary time starts, not asked on Wednesday.

Step 2 · Sunday

Ask only when the calendar actually forks

The agent already knows the Episcopal liturgical calendar. This Wednesday it offers the exact choice from the call: All Saints or Proper 26.

Fr. Thomas picks All Saints. Readings, Collect, and liturgical color update. Track 2 stays on the parish profile and is not mentioned.

If this were the First Sunday of Advent, the agent would only confirm the day. Year would already have flipped from A to B.

Conversational weekly assistant. One question, two honest options, then it gets out of the way.
Step 3 · Cover

Upload, reuse last year, or generate with AI

Drop in a parish photo, reuse last year’s All Saints cover, or let the agent generate one for that Sunday. The agent prompts the image for the feast or season. Every generated cover goes through a short review before it lands on the leaflet.

AI cover generation sits behind a paywall. Gift codes can unlock it for early users so free access feels like a gift, not forever free.

Step 4 · Hymns

Public domain inserts. OneLicense drops in.

Public-domain scores from the one or two libraries you share insert automatically when the hymn is free to use. Most parishes reuse the same familiar songs year after year.

For licensed hymns, the priest downloads the JPEG from OneLicense and drops it into the reserved slot. Titles, authors, and the license footer are filled in for them.

Public domain when we can. OneLicense drop-zone when we must.
Step 5 · Announcements

Rough notes become parish copy

Fr. Thomas pastes the week the way it lives in a notebook. The agent writes them as notices with titles, days, and times. When you share your announcement-writing guide, we train the agent on that voice.

Calendar ingest from the parish website or Google Calendar is in Phase 1 stretch.

Step 6 · Preview

See the leaflet, fix a line, then approve

Before anything is final, Fr. Thomas sees the assembled bulletin. He can correct a title, a time, or a hymn number. The priest always approves before print.

A fuller split-screen layout editor is included in Phase 1 stretch when the simple preview is not enough.
