The brief

Why this, and why now

You called bulletin-making the unholy work of being a priest. The point of this agent is to take that work so the priest can visit hospitals, bless people, and be with the congregation.

What changes every week

  • Readings from the Revised Common Lectionary
  • Cover image
  • Songs / hymns
  • Announcements

What stays in the template

  • Order of service and other static liturgy
  • Collect and weekly propers that appear on your samples
  • Parish defaults: name, translation, OneLicense number, season track
  • Two footers: music license and AI disclosure
How the calendar actually asks questions. In ordinary time, if a major feast overlaps a Sunday, the agent asks the question from the call: All Saints' Day or Proper 26? Track 1 vs Track 2 is chosen at the start of the season and stored on the parish. Outside ordinary time — Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter — there is no fork. The agent confirms the day and moves on.

Step 1 · Sunday

Ask only when the calendar actually forks

The agent already knows the Episcopal liturgical calendar. This Wednesday it offers the exact choice from the call: All Saints or Proper 26.

Fr. Thomas picks All Saints. Readings, Collect, and liturgical color update. Track 2 stays on the parish profile and is not mentioned.

If this were the First Sunday of Advent, the agent would only confirm the day. Year would already have flipped from A to B.

Conversational weekly assistant. One question, two honest options, then it gets out of the way.

Step 3 · Hymns

Name the hymns. Drop the JPEGs.

OneLicense does not offer a public developer API, and logging in with a parish password would break publisher terms. Phase 1 does what you described on the call: the priest downloads the JPEG and puts it in the reserved slot.

Titles, authors, and the license footer are filled in for them. We are not promising a six-minute bulletin. The questions are quick. Licensed scores still take the usual OneLicense download. The saving is the assembly.

A public-domain hymnal catalog can insert scores automatically later if you want that in stretch.

Assisted OneLicense workflow. Legal, low-friction, and honest about the remaining download.
Step 4 · Announcements

Rough notes become parish copy

Fr. Thomas pastes the week the way it lives in a notebook. The agent writes them as notices with titles, days, and times.

Calendar ingest from the church website or Google Calendar is stretch. If you want it in Phase 1, we will pull upcoming events automatically. Otherwise the priest keeps pasting bullets.

Step 5 · Preview

See the leaflet, fix a line, then approve

Before anything is final, Fr. Thomas sees the assembled bulletin. He can correct a title, a time, or a hymn number. The priest always approves before print. That is the trust rule for Phase 1.

A fuller split-screen layout editor is stretch. January needs this: a clear preview and light inline edits, not a design suite.

Phase 2

After clergy trust the weekly job

None of this is required for the January launch. This is why it is a communications agent.

Sunday sermon clips, drafts first

Modeled on the Opus Clip workflow you showed, and on the Virginia priest who already uses it.

After a service that is already going out on YouTube or Facebook Live, the agent finds the sermon, pulls the strongest moments, adds captions, and can add a little music. Clips are saved as drafts. The priest reviews and posts.

When they trust it, they can let it post. You said he is not there yet. We will not start there.

Newsletter

Once that week’s bulletin is approved, the same material can become an email newsletter. If you want this in Phase 1, say so on the markup list below.

Press release

A short interview — a few questions about the feeding program, the festival, the event — then a draft in the format they want, with the same AI disclosure.

Toward January

Eight weeks, if the samples arrive first

This is an eight-week build to a parish-ready Phase 1. Stretch items you pull into Phase 1 land in weeks 5–6 if they are small, or they get their own short pass after the first leaflet is stable.

Week 0
Your sample bulletins

You send 4–6 real leaflets. We lock the default template and the weekly variables from those pages.

Weeks 1–2
Calendar and lectionary

Parish accounts, liturgical calendar, RCL database (Years A, B, C, propers, feast days, Track 1 / Track 2 as a season setting), scripture retrieval.

Weeks 3–4
The Wednesday conversation

Weekly chat, cover upload, hymn titles and OneLicense drop-zone, announcement writing.

Weeks 5–6
Template, preview, PDF

Template from your samples, preview and light edit, print-ready PDF, both compliance footers.

Weeks 7–8
Clergy pilots and January

Real bulletin tests with a few clergy, layout fixes, production deploy for Q1.
